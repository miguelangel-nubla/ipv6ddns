# Reviews

What a review of the whole project looked at, in November 2026: what it found fine, what it left open, and what it fixed. It is here so that the next review does not look into the same things again.

- **Before looking into something that seems wrong**, search this page for it. What is under *Checked and fine* was looked into and is right as it is, and each entry names the test or the comment that says so in the code.
- **Under *Open questions*** is what was left as it is, on purpose, with what would settle it. These are the places to start from.
- **Under *Fixed*** is what was wrong, with the test that holds it now. A test named here must not be removed, nor loosened, without reading its entry.
- **When you look into something and find it fine**, add it to *Checked and fine* of its part, and record why at the code: a test where one is cheap, a comment otherwise. When you fix something, its test goes with the fix, and a line goes here.

## The resolver: `pkg/resolver`

### Checked and fine

- **No failover on SERVFAIL/REFUSED from the first upstream.** By design, documented ("The first answer, whatever its code, is relayed"). `TestUpstreamFailover`.
- **UDP answers sent up to the client's EDNS size.** Documented. `TestTruncation`.
- **Names a zone doesn't have go upstream, not NXDOMAIN.** Documented. `TestAnswers`.
- **A parent zone's record under a deeper zone is never answered.** Intended; comment at `reverse.go:indexReverse`, `TestGivenNames`.
- **Records keyed without lower-casing while `lookup` lower-cases.** `ParseRecord` lower-cases every owner. Comment at `table.go:build`; `TestParseRecord` (`Printer` becomes `printer`).
- **IPv4-mapped addresses.** Unmapped everywhere. `TestDDREntriesOfSockets` ("IPv4-mapped"), `TestDDRHintsOf`.
- **Expired addresses.** Clamped to 1s. `TestCapTTL`, `TestAnswerTTLCappedByExpiry`.
- **CNAME loops.** Refused at load, bounded by `MaxChain`. `TestCheck`, `TestLongChain`.
- **`isAnswerTo` doesn't compare the class, and no 0x20.** Connected UDP socket, random port and id. Comment at `upstream.go:isAnswerTo`; `TestAnswerIsOfItsQuestionOnly`, `TestQueryEchoedIsNoAnswer`.
- **What the cache keeps.** `TestCache`, `TestCacheNegative`, `TestCacheTruncation`.
- **No bailiwick or DNSSEC validation of upstream answers.** Documented ("The cache adds no validation"); comment at `cache.go:put`.
- **DoT pool timer races.** `TestEncryptedShutdownCloses`, `TestDoTReconnect`.
- **The DoT dial deadline covers the handshake.** `tls.Dialer` applies it. Comment at `upstream.go:askTLS`; new `TestDoTSilentHandshake` in `encrypted_test.go`.
- **Bootstrap look-ups never under the status mutex.** `TestUpstreamByNameStatusDoesNotWait`, `TestUpstreamByNameKept`.
- **TSIG MAC verified before the time.** miekg/dns does so, so BADTIME is only signed for a right MAC. Comment at `update.go:serveSigned`; `TestUpdateSignatures`, new case "a wrong secret, an hour ago" (BADSIG, unsigned).
- **Truncated MACs.** Compared whole. Comment at `update.go:keyring.Verify`; new `TestSignatureWhole` in `update_test.go`.
- **No TSIG replay cache.** By choice, as most servers: a replay within the fudge makes again a change its key may make. Comment at `update.go:tsigFudge`.
- **TSIG not last, or over DoH/DoQ.** Refused. `TestSignedOverEncryptedRefused`, `TestUpdateWithoutKeys`.
- **TSIG on a shared DoT socket.** Refused unless the opener's. `TestServeSharedSigned`.
- **TTL of a present record not updated; no RFC 2136 CNAME rule.** Documented; no grant holds CNAMEs. Comment at `update.go:update`.
- **Prerequisites see the grant's records alone.** By design. `TestUpdatePrerequisites`.
- **A signed query in scope is answered from the grant alone.** Documented. `TestSignedQueries`, `TestSignedQueryBetweenIsForwarded`.
- **A key can't widen the query ACL.** `TestUpdateAllow`.
- **`warned` map growth.** Reset past 4096; comment at `update.go:shouldWarn`, `TestUpdateRefusedSignaturesLogged`.
- **Oversize updates.** `TestUpdateTooBig`.
- **A slow TLS handshake doesn't block the DoT accept loop.** `TestSilentTCPClients`, `TestManyClients`.
- **`started.Wait()` can't hang.** The sockets are bound before the servers are started. Comment at `server.go:Start`.
- **DoH client address from `RemoteAddr` alone.** `TestServeEncryptedRefused`, `TestServeSocketAllow`.
- **DoQ message id must be 0; no 0-RTT.** `TestServeQUICProtocolError`; comment at `serve_encrypted.go:serveQUIC`.
- **TLS session tickets per certificate.** Each certificate has its own config and ticket keys. Comment at `shared.go:tlsConfig`.
- **DDR and `resolver.arpa`.** `TestDDR`, `TestDDRWithoutEncrypted`.
- **Deadlocks.** `Status` holds the mutex throughout and nothing it calls takes it again; comment at `server.go:Status`. The new `ddrEntries` accessor is not called from it.

### Open questions

- **HTTP/2 connection coalescing across listeners on a shared `https://` socket.** Unchanged. A request is routed by the handshake's server name whatever its Host, so a browser that reuses one connection for two listeners' names of one certificate has the second's queries answered by the first's resolver. What would settle it: a test with an HTTP/2 client that coalesces (one `http2.Transport` connection, two authorities) against two listeners of one certificate, and the owner's decision whether to answer `421 Misdirected Request` when the Host names another route. The 421 costs clients that send a Host other than the server name on purpose.
- **Routine DHCP conflict checks are logged as warnings** (`update.go:update`, a prerequisite that doesn't hold). Unchanged. What would settle it: a count from `TestLiveUpdatesKea`, or a real Kea, of how many YXDOMAIN/NXRRSET a normal renewal makes, and whether the owner wants them at info, or counted apart from `refused`.
- **The other choice.** An upstream BADCOOKIE over UDP could be asked again over TCP, or with a cookie, before the server counts as failed. Not done: no upstream at hand that requires cookies. A resolver that does (BIND with `require-server-cookie`) in the live tests would settle whether it matters.
- **The cost of a silent kept connection.** A kept connection gone silent now takes up to twice the server's time, 4 seconds, before the next server. A shorter time for the kept try would cut it, at the price of giving up on a slow answer.
- **Live tests.** `pkg/resolver` has no `TestLive`. The default allow of a port alone is held on a real link by the case "ports alone, without allow" of `TestLiveResolverDDR` in `cmd/ipv6ddns/live_resolver_ddr_test.go`: the case before it sets `allow` to everyone, which is why it never saw the bug. Seen failing against the old families, of the bound address, in a copy of the sources: "ports alone, without allow" and "ports alone" fail, an IPv4 client getting no `ipv4hint`, and refused without `allow`; and before them `TestPortAloneDefaultAllow` and `TestDDREntriesOfSockets`, which `tools/live-test.sh` runs first. Still missing there: a case with `[::]` entries, asked over IPv6, which must have no `ipv4hint`. The closed QUIC sockets have no live home: the process exits at shutdown in every live test, which closes the socket anyway.
- **The AD bit is not in the cache key.** An answer fetched for a query with AD set can be served, with its AD bit, to one without. As before this work; harmless to a client that ignores AD, and not a finding of the review.

### Fixed

- **A port-alone listener refused every IPv4 client by default.** The default allow re-derived the family from the bound address, and a port alone is bound to `[::]` as `[::]` is. Each listened address now carries the families it is listened over (`listenedAddr`, `listenedAt`), from the entry's own address. The mirror image in DDR is fixed with it: an explicit `[::]` entry is IPv6 alone, and no longer hinted to IPv4 clients; a port alone is both. Files: `server.go` (`listenedAddr`, `listenedAt`, `listenedNetworks`, `Start`), `ddr.go` (`ddrEntries`), `serve_encrypted.go` (`encryptedListeners.open`), `shared.go` (`sharedAddr` comment). Tests: `TestPortAloneDefaultAllow` in `resolver_test.go`; `TestDDREntriesOfSockets` in `ddr_test.go` (cases "a port alone", "[::]: all of IPv6 alone", "0.0.0.0 and [::], apart").
- **A resolver was served through a shared socket before its own Start, and after a failed one: a data race on `s.ddr`, and a nil dereference with an encrypted upstream.** A `started` state, set under the mutex once Start has all it answers with, is checked where `stopping` was (`answering()` replaces `isStopping()`: DoT shared socket, DoH, the QUIC connection). The upstream clients (`dot`, `doh`) are made in `New` (`openUpstreams`). `s.ddr` is read under the mutex (`Server.ddrEntries`). Files: `server.go`, `shared.go`, `serve_encrypted.go`, `ddr.go`, `upstream.go`. Tests: `TestServeSharedBeforeStart` in `shared_test.go` (not answered before the start, nor after a start that failed, answered after; asked all the while it starts, under `-race`); `TestEncryptedUpstreamBeforeStart` in `encrypted_test.go`. `TestServeSharedSigned` now starts its servers, since one not started is handed no client.
- **An answer to an RD=0 query was served from the cache to RD=1 clients.** RD is in the cache key. Files: `cache.go`. Test: `TestCacheRecursionDesired` in `cache_test.go`.
- **Shutdown left the UDP sockets of DNS over QUIC open.** They are kept (`quicSockets`) and closed after the listeners and the connections. Files: `server.go`, `serve_encrypted.go`. Test: `TestEncryptedShutdown` in `serve_encrypted_test.go`, extended: the UDP address can be listened on again.
- **EDNS was passed through, both ways.** The query sent upstream is built by the resolver (`upstreamQuery`): the question, the RD, AD and CD bits, and, when the client has EDNS, the resolver's OPT: size 1232, the client's DO, padding when encrypted, no option of the client's. The forwarded answer gets a fresh OPT as cached and local ones do (`relayed`), with only an Extended DNS Error (RFC 8914) carried over from upstream. `unpadded` is gone: `relayed` does its work. Files: `server.go`, `upstream.go`. Tests: `TestEDNSHopByHop` in `resolver_test.go` (cookie, client subnet and NSID don't reach the upstream; forwarded, cached and local answers have the same EDNS; an EDE is carried over, and not to a client without EDNS); `TestEDNS` changed (the upstream gets 1232, not the client's 3000). `TestDoT`, `TestDoH`, `TestPadded`, `TestTruncation`, `TestCache`, `TestServeLargeForwardedAnswer` hold unchanged.
- **NXDOMAIN for an empty non-terminal of a private reverse zone with names below it.** `table.reverseAt` tells whether a name is at or above something the resolver answers: an address with a name (tasks', templates', A/AAAA records'), a written record, or a zone of the reverse below. Such a name is NOERROR with no data. Files: `server.go`, `reverse.go` (`reversePrefix`, `reverseAt`, `reverses`; `reverseAddr` is now `reversePrefix` of a whole address), `private.go` (comment). Tests: `TestPrivateEmptyNonTerminal` in `private_test.go` (above a task's address, IPv4 and IPv6, above a record's, above a written PTR, in a zone of the reverse, and the names beside each, which stay NXDOMAIN); `TestPrivateReverse`'s assertion changed, with the two NXDOMAIN cases that remain right.
- **A kept DoT connection gone silent cost the query.** The retry on a new connection has a deadline of its own. Files: `upstream.go` (`askTLS`). Test: `TestDoTSilentKeptConnection` in `encrypted_test.go`.
- **BADTIME carried the server's time and fudge.** It is signed with the client's time signed and fudge, the server's time in Other Data, RFC 8945 5.2.3; the comment says so. Files: `update.go`. Tests: `TestUpdateBadTime` in `update_test.go` (time, fudge, other data, and the MAC made again over the request's MAC and the response); `TestUpdateSignatures` comment fixed. **About miekg/dns's client:** `dns.Client.Exchange` returns `dns: bad authentication` (`ErrAuth`) for this response before and after the fix. Its `stripTsig` refuses any NOTAUTH response before it looks at the signature, so it can verify no BADTIME at all; the test therefore sends the message by hand and recomputes the MAC with `dns.TsigGenerate`.
- **A name given addresses didn't stop the wildcard above it from answering names below it.** `names.owner` stops at a parent that has addresses now. Files: `table.go`. Test: `TestGivenNameStopsWildcard` in `given_test.go` (and the wildcard answers again once the addresses are gone).
- **An EDNS version other than 0 was answered normally.** It is answered BADVERS with an OPT of version 0, after the allow check. Files: `server.go` (`answer`). Test: `TestEDNSVersion` in `resolver_test.go`.
- **A DoQ answer past 65535 bytes was sent with a wrapped length; a DoH upstream body past it was read cut short.** The DoQ answer is truncated as over TCP; the DoH body is refused. Files: `serve_encrypted.go` (`answerQUIC`), `upstream.go` (`askHTTPS`). Tests: `TestServeQUICAnswerTooLarge` in `serve_encrypted_test.go`, `TestDoHAnswerTooLarge` in `encrypted_test.go`.
- **An upstream answer with an extended rcode ended in a silent drop for a client without EDNS** (and, after A5, would have on every transport). Decision: it is not mapped to its low 4 bits, which would be wrong (BADVERS, 16, would read NOERROR with no data; BADCOOKIE, 23, YXRRSET). miekg/dns's `Pack` returns `ErrExtendedRcode` without an OPT, and RFC 6891 gives no way to tell such a code to a client without EDNS. These codes are about the resolver's own query (its EDNS, a cookie, a signature), so the server counts as failed (`extendedCode`): the next is asked, and of none the client gets SERVFAIL, with EDNS or without. Files: `server.go` (`exchange`, `extendedCode`). Test: `TestUpstreamExtendedCode` in `resolver_test.go`.
- **The Grant comment said every method is called one at a time.** It now says what the code does: `Records` and `Apply` are serialised (under `updateMutex`); `Kind`, `Scope` and `Types` are called at any time, concurrently. Checked in `updates.go` (read only): `namesGrant` and `addressesGrant` return constants from them, `challengeGrant.Scope` takes its own mutex, so all three are safe. Files: `update.go` (comment only).
- **The page and the comments said what the code did not.** `Shutdown`'s comment names the QUIC sockets. "Private networks" says a name above a named address gets "no data". "The cache" names RD beside DO and CD, and says the cached answer has the resolver's EDNS. "Forwarded queries" says EDNS is the resolver's own on each side, what goes upstream, what comes back, and what an extended code does. "Answers too big for UDP" has BADVERS.

### What a user can notice

- A resolver listening on a port alone (`dns://53`, `tls://853`) answers the machine's IPv4 networks by default, as the documentation said; before, only the IPv6 ones.
- DDR: an entry on `[::]` no longer gives IPv4 hints; a port alone gives both, as before.
- A client's EDNS options (cookie, client subnet, NSID, keepalive) are no longer forwarded upstream, and the upstream server's are no longer relayed back, except an Extended DNS Error. The size sent upstream is the resolver's 1232 bytes, not the client's.
- Forwarded answers carry the resolver's EDNS (size 1232, the client's DO bit), the same as cached and local answers.
- An upstream server that answers with an extended code (BADVERS, BADCOOKIE and the like) counts as failed: the next server is asked, or the client gets SERVFAIL. Before, the code was relayed, or the answer dropped.
- A query with an EDNS version other than 0 gets BADVERS.
- A query without the RD bit is cached apart from one with it.
- In a private reverse zone, a name above an address that has a name (or above a written record) is NOERROR with no data, not NXDOMAIN.
- A wildcard no longer answers names below a name that currently has addresses given by a task's template.
- The TSIG BADTIME response is signed with the client's time and fudge, so a client with a wrong clock can verify it and read the server's time.
- A DoT upstream connection that went silent no longer fails the query: it is retried on a new connection (up to twice the 2 seconds for that server).
- On a port shared by listeners, a client of a resolver that hasn't started yet (or failed to) has its connection closed (DoT, DoQ) or gets 503 "the resolver isn't answering" (HTTPS, its web server's paths too) instead of an answer. The 503's text was "the resolver is shut down".
- Shutdown frees the UDP port of DNS over QUIC.
- A DoQ answer past 65535 bytes is truncated (`TC`); a DoH upstream answer past it is a failure of that server.

## The worker: the root package

### Checked and fine

- **A retry storm?** No. `TestHostnameChangesWaitForTheRetry`, `TestHostnameRetryHasAFloor`, `TestTemplatesAtAProviderThatLimits`. (The one hole, a change queued during the failing update, is under Fixed.)

### Open questions

- **At the worker's level.** The review's worker-level test (`TemplateNameForgottenBeforeItsRemoval`) depends on which waiter gets the endpoint's mutex, and skips otherwise; it was not added. The `Hostname`-level test is exact, and `expandTemplates` only asks `idle()`.
- **An update asked for with a delay during a failing one becomes a retry.** Not changed: only `ScheduleUpdate(0)` is called today, and that one runs (an asked update goes past both the "already sent" and the retry checks). If a delayed one is ever used, the failure branch of `Hostname.update` should leave a scheduled asked update alone.
- **What remains by design.** Until Route 53's zone is learned, a host's `nas` from a template and a written `nas.example.com` are two names, and the template writes first. Once the zone is known the template's name is stopped, with nothing sent, and the written name sends its addresses again; if the written name never had an address, what the template wrote stays until it has one. `docs/hostnames.md` now says to write the name without its zone there. Likewise a template's name made before the zone was learned that turns out to be the zone itself is emptied, which removes the address records it wrote at the zone's own name.
- **Two *written* spellings of one name at Route 53 are still two `Hostname`s**, each writing the records with its own addresses, as the start's warning says. Merging them would need `w.targets` to change while running (it has no lock, and Home Assistant's entities are made from it at start). Only the metrics are fixed.
- **A file of before that two grants shared.** Each of them reads it, so after the upgrade each has the other's left-over values too, and removes them at their expiry, an hour after they were written. Telling them apart would need each grant's names in full, which Route 53 only knows by asking AWS. The file held only the last writer's records in that case anyway.
- **In `config/`:** `config/updates.go` could refuse a key that names one zone in two `names` permissions, as it does two `challenges` of one credential. The root package no longer needs it.
- **The warning of an expiry that failed changed its end** (`: it is tried again in 1m0s`). No page or test outside the root package quoted the old text.
- **No live test was needed:** nothing here changes what a socket does. `GOOS=linux go vet` passes with the `integration` and `live` tags; the live tests were run later, with `tools/live-test.sh`, and pass. `TestLive…` tests that read `challenges-…json` by name: none do.

### Fixed

- **`writeJSON`'s fixed `.tmp` name, and no fsync.** It was left by choice, as an empty file after a power cut is an error at the start; but the start then stops, and a router has power cuts. Fixed with the push file's: see the configuration's area.
- **A template's name forgotten with its removal still waiting.** `Hostname.idle()` told "nothing left to do" by the times, which also held while the removal had fired and waited for the endpoint, so the name was stopped and its records stayed at the provider for good. Now it goes by a change counter: `updatedChanges`, the count of the addresses' changes the last successful update sent, must equal `addrsChanges`. Files: `hostname.go`. Test: `TestHostnameNotIdleWithARemovalWaiting` in `worker_test.go`.
- **One provider call for each change that came during a slow update.** Every timer that fired queued an update of its own, each sending the same addresses of the end. An update that was not asked for now returns without calling the provider when the last successful update already sent the current change count. Files: `hostname.go`. Test: `TestHostnameChangesWhileAnUpdateRunsAreOneUpdate` in `worker_test.go`.
- **Found while there: a change during a failing update went past the retry.** Its timer had fired and waited behind the update that then failed, so it ran at once after the failure, with no pause; with a debounce of zero or a provider that fails by timing out, that is the usual case. A queued update that is not asked for now leaves it to the retry the failure scheduled. Files: `hostname.go`. Test: `TestHostnameChangeDuringAFailureWaitsForTheRetry` in `worker_test.go`.
- **"Update now" removed the records of names with no address yet.** `ScheduleUpdate` sent whatever the name had, so pressed just after a start it sent an update with no address for every name. It now schedules nothing for a name that never had an address; one that had addresses and lost them still sends the empty update. Files: `hostname.go`, `worker.go` (comment of `updateNow`), `docs/home-assistant-integration.md` (the button's row, and the automation's bullet). Tests: `TestHostnameScheduleUpdateOnlyOnceItHadAddresses` in `worker_test.go`, `TestUpdateNowBeforeAnyAddress` in `worker_e2e_test.go`.
- **Templates at a provider that learns its zone later (Route 53).** The names were kept by the name of their records, which changes from `printer` to `printer.example.com` at the first update: a second `Hostname` was made, the first was emptied and removed the records of a present host, and a name written was no longer found, so a template wrote over it. Design chosen, explained in the comment of `Worker.rename`: at each matching the endpoint's names and `w.dynamic` are keyed again by the names of now, so each `Hostname` stays; the record name of each spelling is read once for the round; two template names that turn out to be one become one (the other is stopped with nothing sent, and the one kept sends its addresses again). `w.dynamic` now holds `*dynamicName` (the `Hostname` and its spelling), and `newHostname` takes the name it was looked up by, so that it can't be read twice. The warning of a name written is told once by its text, so at Route 53 it is told once before the zone is learned and once after, with the name in full. Files: `worker.go`, `docs/hostnames.md` (a paragraph on Route 53 under "A name written wins"). Tests: `TestTemplateAtAProviderThatLearnsItsZone`, `TestWrittenNameWinsAtAProviderThatLearnsItsZone`, `TestTemplatesThatAreOneNameOnceTheZoneIsLearned` in `worker_templates_test.go`.
- **Duplicate metric series for two spellings of one name.** `nas` and `nas.example.com` at a Route 53 endpoint had the same `hostname` label once the zone was learned, and Prometheus refuses a scrape with a series twice. Names of one endpoint that share a domain are now labelled by their spelling; all others by their domain, as before. Files: `status.go` (`WriteMetrics`). Test: `TestMetricsOfTwoNamesOfOneDomain` in `worker_e2e_test.go`.
- **A template naming the zone itself.** With a single-label zone such as `lan`, a device calling itself `lan` got its addresses written to the zone's own records, since `ddns.FQDN("lan", "lan")` is `lan`. `expandTemplates` leaves out an expansion whose record name is that of `""` at the endpoint, warned once; a name written in a task is untouched. Files: `worker.go`, `docs/hostnames.md`. Test: `TestTemplateNeverNamesTheZone` in `worker_templates_test.go`.
- **Provider errors served with the sensible data hidden.** A certificate's `renewal_error` and `error`, and the `last_message` of a key's update that failed, carried the provider's or the ACME authority's error on `/status.json` and the web page. They are now `<sensible data hidden>` there, and whole in the unhidden view; the metric of a failed renewal still says 1. Only a *failed* update's message is hidden: what an update did and why one was refused are the resolver's own words. Files: `certificates.go` (`certificateStatuses(hideSensible)`), `worker.go` (`resolverStatuses(hideSensible)`, the two pages), `status.go`. Tests: `TestCertificateErrorHidden` in `certificates_test.go`, `TestUpdatesKeyErrorHidden` in `updates_test.go`. `TestUpdatesChallenges` now reads the key's message from the unhidden status.

### What a user can notice

Each of these refuses, or answers differently, something that loaded or worked before.

- **A source with a key of another type** is an error: `urls` outside http, `command` and `args` outside command, `addresses` outside static, `entity` and `attribute` outside homeassistant. Before, only `urls` of a dns source, `family`, `password` and `state_file` were. Message: `source s: addresses is only for static sources, and this one's type is http, which doesn't read it: leave it out, or make the type static`.
- **A static source with `interval` or `lifetime`** is an error. Before, both were replaced with 1h and 3h without a word. Message: `source s: a static source has no interval nor lifetime: its addresses are always those written. Leave both out`.
- **A push source's `lifetime` under 1s** is an error: `source s: lifetime 1ns is too short: the least is 1s. ...`.
- **A push source's name** with a comma, `/`, `\`, a space around it, or that is `.` or `..`, is an error: `sources: the name "a,b" of a push source is the hostname its router sends, and in the name of its file: use one without a comma, a slash or a space around it, as wan`. Sources of other types keep any name.
- **Two push sources of one file** are an error, also when their names, or their files, differ by case alone: `sources.b.state_file: push.json is the file of the push source a too, and each would read the other's addresses at a restart: give one of them a file of its own, as state_file: push-b-2.json`.
- **A web server whose listeners have no http:// or https:// entry** is an error: `webservers.main.listeners: lan has no http:// nor https:// entry, so the web server would serve nowhere: add one to its listen, as http://8053, or name a listener that has one`.
- **A web server's socket on the address and port of another's, or of a resolver's plain DNS or DNS over TLS entry**, is an error at the reading, where it was "address already in use" at the start: `listeners.b.listen: http://127.0.0.1:8053 is on the address and port of listeners.a's http://127.0.0.1:8053, two sockets that can't both listen: give one of them another port, or leave one out`.
- **An unquoted name that YAML reads as another** is an error, anywhere in the file: `y`, `n`, `yes`, `no`, `on`, `off` in any case, and numbers not written as YAML writes them back, as `010`, `0x10`, `1_000`, `1.10`. Before, the source `y` loaded as the source `true`. Message: `sources.y: YAML reads the name y, written without quotes, as the boolean true, and it would be named true: write it in quotes, as "y"`. An unquoted `true`, `false`, `10` or `1.5` still loads, as that name.
- **`certificates.<name>.acme.directory` that is not an http:// or https:// URL** is an error: `certificates.home.acme.directory: "not a url" is not a URL of http:// or https://: give the authority's directory, as https://acme-v02.api.letsencrypt.org/directory, or leave it out for Let's Encrypt's`.
- **Under ACME, `certificate` and `key` as one file**, or either as `acme-account.key` beside the certificate, is an error: `certificates.home: certificate and key are one file, ...`. Without ACME one file for both is still read, as before.
- **Two blocklists whose names differ by case alone** are an error: `blocklists: the names Pro and pro differ by their case alone, ...`.
- **A push without `myip` from an address that is not public** gets 400 and `911` where it got `good`: from 100.64.0.0/10, 198.18.0.0/15, 192.0.0.0/24, 0.0.0.0/8, 240.0.0.0/4, the documentation ranges, 64:ff9b:1::/48, 100::/64, 2001:db8::/32 and 3fff::/20. With `myip` nothing changes.
- **A POST to `/nic/update` with a body over 64 KiB** gets 413 and `911`. The limit was 10 MB.
- **A web request** is ended when its headers and body take more than 30s to come, when its answer takes more than a minute, and a connection with no request is closed after 2 minutes. When stopping, requests still in progress after 5s are cut off; they had up to 30s.
- **The report's hidden view** shows an ACME directory, and a credential's `endpoint`, as scheme and host alone. Anything that read the path from `/status.txt` or the page no longer finds it.

- **No `ExecReload`.** The program handles only SIGINT/SIGTERM (`cmd/ipv6ddns/ipv6ddns.go:153`); the docs and the comment in `packaging/config.yaml:4` say "restart". Adding `ExecReload=kill -HUP` would kill it.
- **No `AmbientCapabilities`.** It runs as root, so the bounding set suffices. `package-test.sh:55` asserts `CapEff` is exactly `0x2400`, and `nix/test.nix:40-41` asserts the same set.
- **`RestrictAddressFamilies` (`:42`).** `AF_PACKET` and `AF_NETLINK` are present; `package-test.sh:131-150` proves NDP, ping, IPv4, mDNS and DHCP discovery under the unit and greps the journal for refusals. CONTRIBUTING:84 makes this the rule.
- **`ProtectKernelTunables`.** ipv6disc only reads `/proc/sys/net/ipv4/neigh/*` (`pkg/arp/settle_linux.go`) and `/proc/net/if_inet6` (`temporary_linux.go`). Do not add `ProcSubset=pid`; it would hide `/proc/net`. No file records this.
- **`PrivateDevices=yes`.** It does not drop `CAP_NET_RAW`; the `CapEff` assertion proves it.
- **`MemoryDenyWriteExecute`, `SystemCallArchitectures=native`, `LockPersonality`.** Fine for a static `CGO_ENABLED=0` Go binary. No `SystemCallFilter` is set, so nothing there can be too tight. Covered by `systemd-analyze verify` at `package-test.sh:53`.
- **`ReadWritePaths=/etc/ipv6ddns` when the directory is missing** would be a 226/NAMESPACE failure, but nfpm ships it (`.goreleaser.yaml:210-213`, and it is in the `dist/` deb). The Nix module resets the path for this reason, with a comment at `module.nix:110-112`.
- **State files world-readable?** No: every writer uses 0600/0700 (`homeassistant.go:184`, `updates.go:541`, `source/push.go:160`, `pkg/acme`, `pkg/blocklist`), even though `/etc/ipv6ddns` is 0755.
- **`DynamicUser` / ownership across upgrades.** Not used; root-owned throughout.
- **`TimeoutStopSec=40s`** is above the program's 30 s grace, as is OpenRC's `retry="TERM/40/KILL/5"`.
- **`After=network-online.target`** is needed: listeners resolve networks and names at start (`docs/installation.md:206-210`).
- **Unit under `/usr/lib/systemd/system` on Debian** is read on merged-usr and non-merged systems, and tested there.
- **No `MemoryMax`, `TasksMax` or `LimitNOFILE`** is set, so none is too tight.

### Open questions

- **Root without `CAP_DAC_OVERRIDE`.** Under the unit, root has two capabilities, so a `${file:...}` secret or a certificate's key that is another user's, with mode 0600, is "permission denied" though the service runs as root. Not checked under the unit. A sentence in `docs/installation.md` would settle it; `CAP_DAC_READ_SEARCH` should not be added for it.
- **DNS over QUIC under the unit.** quic-go tries `SO_RCVBUFFORCE`, which needs `CAP_NET_ADMIN`, and falls back. If the line it logs says "not permitted", `tools/package-test.sh`'s check of the log would fail once it tests a `quic://` listener. To settle it: add one to the package test's configuration.
- **A purge leaves the state.** `apt purge` removes the configuration file alone: `tls/` (the ACME account's key and the certificates'), `homeassistant.state`, `push-*.json` and `blocklists/` stay in `/etc/ipv6ddns`. Debian's convention is a `postrm purge` that removes them; `packaging/postremove.sh` has none. Left, since it deletes keys: the owner's call.
- **The release's systems.** `js/wasm` was built and can't run: it is no longer. Solaris and Windows stay, as ipv6disc's discovery page says what works there, and CI lints both; `README.md` names them now. MIPS and ARM are built for routers without a floating point unit now: see Fixed.

### Fixed

- **A configuration with an error restarted every 5 seconds, for ever.** Every failure ended with 1, and `RestartSec=5s` never reaches systemd's start limit. A configuration that can't be used, or none found, ends with 78 now, EX_CONFIG (`exitConfig`, through zap's fatal hook), and the unit has `RestartPreventExitStatus=78`, which the NixOS module's unit takes too; any other failure, as a port another program holds, is still 1, and tried again. OpenRC's `supervise-daemon` has no such setting, and restarts it either way. `docs/installation.md` and the manual page's EXIT STATUS say so. Test: `TestConfigErrorExitCode` in `cmd/ipv6ddns/start_e2e_test.go`, seen failing against the old code: exit 1.
- **Test inputs that were not pinned.** `tools/live-providers.sh` downloaded RouterOS with nothing checked, and seven images were pinned by tag alone. Each has its digest now, of the tag's index, OpenWrt's of each target; the zip of RouterOS must be of the SHA-256 written there, the one MikroTik publishes at `chr-<version>.img.zip.sha256`, and another `ROUTEROS_VERSION` needs its `ROUTEROS_SHA256`. Checked: each reference resolves, and the check passes the zip and refuses another hash. The `latest` and `stable` tags are of `tools/package-test.sh` and `tools/nix-test.sh`, and stay: a package is installed on what is released, and the flake pins nixpkgs itself. Their comments say so.
- **`actionlint` had not been run.** actionlint 1.7.12, with its shellcheck of the `run:` scripts, passes all five workflows.
- **Router builds.** MIPS was hardfloat alone: OpenWrt's MIPS kernels don't emulate a floating point unit, and the binary stopped on `Illegal instruction`. `gomips: softfloat` now, which also covers GOMIPS64 and runs on a MIPS that has one. ARM had no GOARM=5, and ARMv6 and ARMv7 stop at the start without VFP, as on the Broadcom routers of Asuswrt-Merlin: `goarm` has 5 now, and CI builds `linux/arm/5`. Checked with qemu-user in Debian: mips, mipsle on `-cpu 24Kc` and mips64 on `5KEc` softfloat run, mipsle hardfloat dies; ARMv5 runs on `arm926` and `cortex-a7`, ARMv7 dies on `arm926`. The archives' names don't say softfloat, as there is no hardfloat one to tell it from. The binary is about 25 MB, 8 MB gzipped: a router of 16 MB of flash can't hold it without USB storage, which `docs/installation.md` says. Not router targets, kept as they cost nothing to build: ppc64, ppc64le, s390x, loong64, riscv64. MikroTik's containers are arm, arm64 and amd64 alone, so MIPS is no help there.

- **The NixOS module wrote its state beside the configuration, which is in the store.** A push source's addresses, ACME's certificates, the blocklists' copies and the dynamic updates' files all failed to be written. `settings` now has `state_dir: /var/lib/ipv6ddns` by default, and the option's text says a `configFile` must set it. Files: `nix/module.nix`, `nix/test.nix`, `docs/installation.md`. Test: the `state_dir` line of `nix/test.nix`.

The unit has `StateDirectory=ipv6ddns` now. Files: `packaging/ipv6ddns.service`, `docs/installation.md`. Test: `tools/package-test.sh` starts the service with that `state_dir`; seen failing without `StateDirectory=`, in Debian, with `state_dir: mkdir /var/lib/ipv6ddns: read-only file system` at every restart.

- **`tools/unifi-os-vm.sh` took a failed setup for a passed one**, by a pipe's status, replaced the variables with an empty file, and then sent that to the machine, where it took the admin's password. It also waited for ever for a machine that never booted.
